Key Historical Details

  • The Royal Visit: In 1889, Queen Margherita of Savoy and King Umberto I visited Naples, desiring local, simple food over French cuisine.
  • The Creator: Raffaele Esposito of Pizzeria Brandi (then known as Pizzeria della Regina d'Italia) was commissioned to create the pizzas.
  • Symbolism: The ingredients (tomato, mozzarella, basil) were chosen to match the colors of the Italian flag.
  • The Letter: A thank-you note from the Queens head of service is still on display in Pizzeria Brandi, although some historians have questioned the authenticity of this story.
  • Historical Doubt: While the legend is popular, research suggests the story and name were primarily promoted in the 1930s-1940s, and similar pizza combinations likely existed before 1889.
  • Despite debates over the exact origin story, the Margherita pizza became an iconic, globally recognized symbol of Naples and traditional Italian cuisine.
  1. Prep Time: 1 hour Cook Time: 7 minutes Total Time: 1 hour 7 minutes Yield: 4 servings (2 slices each) 1x Category: Main Dish Method: Baked Cuisine: Italian
  2. For the pizza dough
  3. 1 ball Best Pizza Dough* (or Thin Crust Dough or KitchenAid Pizza Dough)
  4. Semolina flour or cornmeal, for dusting the pizza peel
  5. For the pizza sauce
  6. 1 small garlic clove (1/2 medium)
  7. 15 ounces crushed fire roasted tomatoes or San Marzano tomatoes
  8. 1 tablespoon extra virgin olive oil
  9. ½ teaspoon dried oregano
  10. ½ teaspoon kosher salt (scant)
  11. For the toppings
  12. 1/4 to 1/3 cup pizza sauce
  13. ¾ cup shredded mozzarella cheese or 2 to 3 ounces fresh mozzarella cheese
  14. Parmesan cheese, for topping (optional)
  15. Fresh basil leaves, for garnish
    16. Instructions
  16. Make the pizza dough: Follow the Best Pizza Dough recipe to prepare the dough. (This takes about 15 minutes to make and 45 minutes to rest.)
  17. Place a pizza stone in the oven and preheat to 500°F. OR preheat your pizza oven (here’s the pizza oven we use).
  18. Make the pizza sauce: Cut the garlic into a few rough pieces. Place the garlic, tomatoes, olive oil, oregano and kosher salt in a blender. Blend until fully combined. (You’ll use about ⅓ cup for the pizza; reserve the remaining sauce and refrigerate for up to 1 week.)
  19. Prepare the cheese: If using fresh mozzarella cheese, slice it into ¼ inch thick pieces (see the photos of the pre-baked pizza above). If it’s incredibly watery fresh mozzarella (all brands vary), you may want to let it sit on a paper towel to remove moisture for about 15 minutes then dab the mozzarella with the paper towel to remove any additional moisture.
  20. Bake the pizza: When the oven is ready, dust a pizza peel with cornmeal or semolina flour. (If you don’t have a pizza peel, you can use a rimless baking sheet or the back of a rimmed baking sheet. But a pizza peel is well worth the investment!) Stretch the dough into a circle; see How to Stretch Pizza Dough for instructions. Then gently place the dough onto the pizza peel.
  21. Spread a thin layer of the pizza sauce over the dough, using about ¼ to ⅓ cup. Add the mozzarella cheese. Top with a thin layer of fresh grated Parmesan cheese (if using) and a few pinches of kosher salt.
  22. Use the pizza peel to carefully transfer the pizza onto the preheated pizza stone. Bake the pizza until the cheese and crust are nicely browned, about 5 to 7 minutes in the oven (or 1 minute in a pizza oven).
